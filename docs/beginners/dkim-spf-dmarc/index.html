DKIM, SPF and DMARC

Three short records protect your name. They let other servers check that a message from you really is from you.

Why they exist

Anyone can type any address into the “From” line of an email, including yours. Without checks, criminals could send messages that look like they come from your shop. These three records are how the world’s mail servers tell real messages from fakes, and they are the biggest factor in whether your email reaches the inbox.

SPF: who may send

SPF is a guest list. It names the servers that are allowed to send mail for your domain. A receiving server looks at who actually delivered the message and checks the list. If the sender is not on it, the message looks suspicious.

DKIM: a seal that proves it was not changed

DKIM works like a wax seal on a letter. Geserd signs every message with a private key that only we hold. The matching public key lives in your DNS. The receiving server uses the public key to check the seal: if the message was altered on the way, the seal no longer fits.

DMARC: what to do when checks fail

DMARC is your instruction to the receiver. It says which of the two checks above must pass and what to do with a message that fails. The record Geserd gives you starts with p=none, which means “watch and report, do not block yet”. That is the safe way to begin. Later, once you are sure all your legitimate mail passes, you can ask receivers to treat failures more strictly.

You do not have to understand the details to benefit. Add the records Geserd shows, press verify, and the protection is in place.
