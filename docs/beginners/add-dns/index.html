Adding DNS records step by step

A walk through the screens you will meet when you paste Geserd’s records into your domain company’s DNS page.

Before you start

  • Know where the DNS lives. It is at the company that manages your domain. If you bought it at one place but moved the name servers to another, such as a website host, the records go to that second place.
  • Open the Domains page in Geserd and leave it in a second tab. You will copy from it.

Adding one record

  • Find the DNS page. Look for “DNS”, “DNS records”, “Zone editor” or “Name servers” in the domain’s settings.
  • Press “Add record”. Choose the type exactly as Geserd shows it, TXT or MX.
  • Fill in the name. Some companies want only the short name (_dmarc), others want the full name (_dmarc.yourshop.com). If one form is rejected, try the other. For the domain itself use @, or leave the field empty.
  • Paste the value. Use the copy button in Geserd and paste. Do not retype it, and do not add quotation marks unless the form asks for them.
  • Leave the lifetime (TTL) alone. The default, often called “Auto” or 3600, is fine.
  • Save. Repeat for each record in the list, then return to Geserd and press verify.

The mistakes that happen most

  • Two SPF records. A domain may have only one record starting with v=spf1. If you already have one from another service, add Geserd’s address to it instead of creating a second.
  • A hidden space or line break at the end of the value. Paste again and check the end of the line.
  • The domain name added twice. Some forms add .yourshop.com themselves, which turns the name into _dmarc.yourshop.com.yourshop.com. If verification fails, try the short name.

How long it takes

Most companies publish a change within a few minutes. In rare cases it takes a few hours, and very rarely up to a day. If Geserd still shows a record as missing after an hour, open the record on your side, compare it character by character with the one in Geserd, and open a ticket if you cannot find a difference.
