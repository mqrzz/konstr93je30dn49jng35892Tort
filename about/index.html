Story

Why ovyrn exists

Email is still the most reliable way to reach people, and still one of the most annoying things to set up correctly. DNS records, sender reputation, bounce handling and inbound parsing: almost every project ends up rebuilding the same plumbing from scratch, usually after something has already gone wrong in production. ovyrn puts that plumbing behind one API, in both directions, so a small team can send and receive real mail without becoming email infrastructure experts first.

  • Started by developers tired of rebuilding the same setup
  • Sending and receiving in one product, not two
  • Built and run on infrastructure we operate ourselves

Principles

What we believe

These are not marketing lines, they are the rules we actually build by. Simple first means we would rather ship one clear way to do something than five configurable ones. Honest by default means the docs and this very page say what works today and what does not, instead of implying everything is finished. Careful with reputation means limits exist even when they are inconvenient, because a shared IP is a shared responsibility.

  • We say what is not finished, not just what is
  • Defaults are safe even when they cost convenience
  • Limits protect deliverability for everyone, not just you

What we look for in a good service

The same standards we hold ourselves to.

Simple first

One API, clear docs, sensible defaults instead of endless configuration.

Honest by default

We say what works today and what is still being built, on this page and in the docs.

Careful with reputation

Limits and guardrails protect deliverability for everyone sharing our infrastructure.

Easy to understand

Infrastructure

Small, on purpose

ovyrn runs on infrastructure we manage ourselves, not on a reseller layer on top of someone else's email service. That means fewer moving parts between your message and the recipient's inbox, but it also means we start small and grow capacity as real usage grows, instead of promising limits we cannot yet support on day one.

  • We operate our own servers, not a resold service
  • Capacity grows with real, paying usage
  • A dedicated IP is planned once volume justifies it
